# Results draft

We evaluated six prespecified within- and cross-resolution settings using 2047 conservative C4/C8 record components, 3 base/novel tasks and 3 seeds. Both 47,400-parameter M1 encoders were trained afresh with matched initialization, group orders and 60-epoch cross-entropy budgets; evaluation-query components were excluded from training and checkpoint selection in both resolutions. All incremental sessions used frozen encoders, 20 base references per class and 1/5/10/20 novel supports, with 5/10-shot conditions designated primary.

Averaged within task/seed over the primary shots, M1 Full achieved 81.90% final accuracy for C4-to-C8 and 82.99% for C8-to-C4 transfer. Relative to the corresponding within-source conditions, changes were -8.99 and -7.93 percentage points. Replacing source-domain base references with target-domain references changed accuracy by +8.98 and +7.63 points, respectively.
Averaged within task/seed over the primary shots, M1 Shrinkage achieved 77.25% final accuracy for C4-to-C8 and 75.28% for C8-to-C4 transfer. Relative to the corresponding within-source conditions, changes were -17.90 and -20.56 percentage points. Replacing source-domain base references with target-domain references changed accuracy by +17.18 and +19.40 points, respectively.

The mean shrinkage-minus-full-covariance contrast ranged from -7.71 to +4.92 percentage points across the six settings, with positive mean contrasts in 4/6 settings. Across the four cross-source settings, PCA64-shrinkage minus M1-shrinkage ranged from +2.20 to +12.83 points. These controls qualify any claim that the neural representation or a single covariance strategy is uniformly preferable.
The neural representation therefore did not show a mean accuracy advantage over the fixed PCA64-shrinkage control in any of the four cross-source settings.

Classifier and source-prior effects are reported separately from reference-bank effects; matched-target 1-NN comparisons isolate the encoder-source contrast without covariance priors. PCA64-shrinkage is included as a fixed non-neural control, with its dimensionality and relative regularization differences disclosed. Novel classes were registered without encoder updates, but unchanged old-class statistics did not guarantee unchanged recognition when new classes entered the decision set. The paired tables and class-specific confusion matrices retain negative transfer and retention outcomes rather than selecting a favorable direction.

These findings are exploratory. Record-component blocking removes known exact duplicates and linked IDs but does not establish physical specimen independence; C4/C8 are not independent instruments, and no genuinely untouched confirmatory dataset was available. Repeated episodes were not treated as independent specimens. Legacy C4-support-to-C8-query results use different checkpoints and splits and were retained separately from target-support registration.
